# Large editorial Reels over fixed photography

Use this treatment when the user selects a fixed photographic background with large information placed directly on the screen, or approves it for future editions. It is a selectable treatment, not a requirement for unrelated accounts. Read the current account brief first; later feedback takes precedence over older motion or panel choices.

## Let the information occupy the screen

Keep a relevant real photograph at a fixed crop, size and position. Use a fixed neutral contrast layer when needed so large type remains readable; retain the image's natural colors. Do not pan, zoom, split or shuffle a still photo just to make the screen change.

Place the explanation directly across the protected central screen area. Avoid confining the story to a small rounded card below a repeated headline. Use large names, functions, comparisons and a few open separators. A heading introduces the question; the main visual answers it. Remove duplicated headings and auxiliary captions when they compete with the answer.

Use the available space rather than scaling a previous card uniformly. In the approved 1080-wide composition, primary explanatory type was about 40–66 px and supporting lines 32–40 px; these are starting ranges, not universal floors. Adapt line breaks and information density to the language and view. At a 4K export, render type natively at the matching scale. Keep important information within the selected UI safe area.

## Calibrate space against the actual Reels screen

When the user provides an uploaded capture, use it to refine the layout. Measure the video canvas against unchanged landmarks such as the brand and heading, then identify the top header, right control rail and bottom account/caption overlay. Do not treat a conservative preset as proof that all space below it is unusable. Extend the photo and information vertically into a demonstrated gap, increase useful row spacing or type size, and move credits and scene progress downward while retaining a margin above the account overlay. Preserve the content rather than adding filler to occupy space.

For the 2026-10-17 Chihuahua AI Lab capture, the old progress indicator at logical y=1415 left an unnecessary lower gap. The user approved the expanded layout for future Reels on 2026-10-17: in a 1080×1920 composition, use an important-content rectangle x=120–890, y=280–1660, information from y=636–1546 and progress at y=1640 as the account's default starting layout. Fill the available lower space with useful explanation spacing and fixed photography, with credits and segmented progress above the account overlay. Do not revert to the older y=1440 cutoff for this account. Adapt the arrangement to each story; this approved, screenshot-informed starting layout is not a universal Instagram boundary or a claim that the revision has been uploaded. Overlay positions can change with device, crop, caption expansion and app version. Recheck them against the latest actual capture.

Create a comparison or overlay preview when it helps assess the revision. Label reused app UI, schematic controls and simulated placements accurately. A local simulation and recompression preview do not establish a new Instagram upload result. Keep the original capture out of a public skill bundle; store only reusable measurement guidance and the selected account margins.

## Visualize information that helps the viewer understand

Before animating a scene, write the question it answers and the source-grounded fact or relationship its layout reveals. Useful choices include company roles, technology names paired with their functions, development targets, and announced versus undisclosed information. A diagram is optional: a spatial comparison or large typographic grouping is often sufficient.

Use quantitative charts only when the source supplies the data. Use process arrows only when the source establishes a process. Do not invent driving paths, performance measurements, live detection boxes or product interfaces to decorate an announcement. Separate development plans from released features and preserve essential qualifications beside the claim.

## Let motion introduce information

For this treatment, use `motion_policy.mode: "information_linked"`. Reveal a new fact once in reading order, retain it, and allow time to read. Use short, soft scene handoffs. Do not add circulating connectors, repeated highlighting sweeps, decorative particles, shaking labels or unrelated object movement to fill a hold.

This preference replaces an earlier request for uninterrupted motion when the user approves the calmer information-led treatment. Record that resolution in the selected brief. Do not revive the old continuous-motion requirement or fail an intentionally fixed photograph during a motion audit. Apply continuous motion only if the latest request explicitly selects it again.

Keep a separate progress segment for each scene when selected; derive the segment count and scene durations from the actual story. A segmented indicator is a navigation aid, not a substitute for an explanation.

## Review and save the treatment

Watch the entire encoded video at normal speed and phone size. Check that the main information is readable, the photograph stays fixed, reveals add actual information, comparisons make sense, and important copy stays clear of Reels controls. Review resized and recompressed derivatives when the brief requires them. Pixel changes and export checks do not establish information quality.

Keep original news facts and historical edition dates when revising. Deliver the MP4 and the existing Reel package items. Store selected account preferences in its brief, and preserve prior versions. Use a saved account profile only for that named account; never import its identity or acknowledgement into an unrelated task.
