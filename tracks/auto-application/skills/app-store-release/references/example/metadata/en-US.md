## Description
Tonlit reads the skin tone in your photo and moves it to the shade you choose, from fair to deep. It is a photo editor built for one job: skin colour.

Read the tone in your photo
• Tonlit measures skin on the forehead, cheeks, chin, under the eyes and on the neck, and shows you where it measured
• Your shade on Tonlit's 15-step scale, from Fair to Rich, with its undertone: cool, neutral or warm
• Light colour, redness, evenness, under-eye brightness and whether your neck matches your face

Set the shade you want
• Pick any shade from 1 to 15. Tonlit moves skin toward it while the background, hair and clothes stay as they are
• Eight recipes, including Bright tone-up, Clear skin, Peach warm, Porcelain, Glass skin, Soft matte and Golden tan
• Copy a tone: bring the skin colour of a photo you like onto yours
• Match many photos: one shade across a set of up to 20 photos

Refine skin, light and details
• Skin: tone-up, undertone, healthy flush, clarity, even tone, redness, blemishes, texture, dark circles, finish and neck tone
• Light: light colour, exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, warmth, tint and saturation
• Details: blush, lips, aegyo-sal and eye definition; V-line, small face, eyes and nose; hair and arms
• Quick clean-up applies the suggested fixes in one tap, and a second tap removes them

Compare colours
• Colour draping holds four seasons of colour up to your face so you can compare them side by side. It is a comparison, not a diagnosis

Private by design
• Photos are edited on your iPhone and never uploaded
• No account, no ads, no subscriptions and no in-app purchases. Every feature is included
• Save the full-size photo or a tone card to your photo library

Shade numbers and undertones describe colour in a photo as an editing guide. They change with light, camera and makeup. They are not a medical or personal colour diagnosis and do not match any brand's foundation shades.

Available in English, Korean, Japanese, Chinese (Simplified and Traditional), Spanish, Portuguese (Brazil), French, German, Thai, Vietnamese and Indonesian.
