SkillMerge: A Conditional Guard for Agreement
Research Forge · 19 October 2026 · Layout v21 · Research content v21
Compact working paper v21, 2026-10-19. New conditional theorem and deterministic follow-up on an exposed bank; no 
independent skill history, native benchmark, model/API call, or professor endorsement. Preserved v20 sources and 
complete v19 scientific record accompany the supplement.
Abstract
Can agreement be promoted without discarding useful evidence? We analyze a reference-blind 
prefix guard on an already exposed SciFact-derived bank, not a behavioral effect of executable 
skills. Fixed round-robin and stable common-item promotion orders share an output capacity. 
The guard accepts promotion when their selected ID sets coincide or promotion preserves either 
complete input. We prove this condition necessary and sufficient for full-containment no-loss over 
every reference family making both complete inputs annotation-sufficient; otherwise the policy falls 
back to round-robin. Among 12,067 such constructed pairs, guarded gain/loss counts are 563/0, 
95/0, and 0/0 at capacities one, two, and three. Conservatism discards two promotion gains, while 
all 22,794 pairs retain 487 losses at capacity one. First-three scoring still fails above capacity three. 
Frozen computation and separate author verification preserve denominators, counterexamples, and 
earlier failed checks. Independent skill histories and qualified behavioral effects remain absent.
1 Introduction
When two skill changes help separately, their joint availability may still reduce performance. Possible 
causes include incompatible requirements, context length, lost evidence, changed ranking, or a 
restrictive grader. We first isolate the last three mechanisms using fixed evidence-ID lists. Agreement 
denotes repeated IDs, not semantic agreement or independently sampled reasoning. Figure 1 separates 
this executed diagnostic lane from the still unexecuted skill-authoring and behavioral-evaluation lane.
The question is which reference-blind switch between round-robin and common-item promotion can 
guarantee no full-containment regression, assuming each complete input already contains a valid 
reference alternative. This assumption enables a stronger guarantee than unrestricted references, but 
is not independently observed usefulness of executable skills. We prove the exact structural boundary, 
evaluate its conservative fallback, and retain the unprotected all-pair losses.

2 Related Work
SciFact and FEVER tie correctness to reference evidence [1,3]. The pinned SciFact scorer inspects 
only three supplied rationale IDs for rationalized abstract correctness [2]. ERASER separates 
human-rationale agreement from predictive faithfulness [4]; our annotation containment is not its 
confidence-based sufficiency. CheckList motivates focused invariance and directional tests [5], here of 
deterministic contracts.
Multi-metric evaluation [6,7], construct validity [8,9], underspecification [11], documentation [12], 
benchmark selection [13], and selection bias [21] motivate explicit score-to-claim boundaries. DSPy 
compilation [10], SkillCommit replay [14], SkillSmith migration [15], SkillCAT assembly [16], and 
SkillEvoReg regression tests [17] are substantive unexecuted comparisons. Resource controls [18], 
SkillsBench v1.1 [19], and self-consistency [20] use different information and budgets; sampled-answer 
voting is not our ID promotion. All 21 inherited references retain their inspection scopes. New 
prospective benchmark reading is separated in Supplement S22.
3 Problem and Method
References form a finite nonempty family of nonempty ID sets. Full containment passes when any 
complete alternative occurs anywhere in an ordered output. First-three applies containment only to 
its first three IDs; exact-annotated requires equality with an enumerated alternative. Distinct, in-
range input lists A and B are scanned alternately, retaining first occurrences, to produce the complete 
round-robin order V. Stable promotion P moves common IDs first and preserves V order within both 
classes; both outputs are capped at k.
Write X and Y for their selected ID sets. The guard below reads A, B, k only. If it accepts, H returns 
promotion; otherwise it returns round-robin. References enter only subsequent scoring and descriptive 
stratification. The conditional reference class requires some alternative wholly inside each complete 
input, possibly different alternatives. It does not require each k-truncated input to pass.
For every family in that class, promotion cannot lose full containment exactly when G=1. Sufficiency 
follows from identical selected sets or retention of an entire sufficient input. Conversely, if all three 
tests fail, choose a removed baseline ID and an ID outside Y in each input. At most two singleton 
alternatives make both inputs sufficient, the baseline successful, and promotion unsuccessful. Supple­
ment S16 proves this reverse construction and its quantifiers.
Thus the guard is maximal only among binary P-or-V switches promising safety for this conditional 
reference class. It may reject a gain under the actual annotations. For input lengths at most d, where 
d is at least two, capacity at least 2d-2 always satisfies the guard; a common-last construction shows 
sharpness at 2d-3. The earlier length-three/cap-four result is a special case. Empty inputs and cap 
zero are explicit endpoints. No-loss allows both outputs to fail.

4 Experimental Setup
The unchanged bank contains 309 evidence-bearing tasks, 204 recorded components, 2,494 distinct 
authored inputs, and 22,794 ordered pairs. Reversed and diagonal pairs are constructed views, not 
independent histories. Seven capacities yield 159,558 decisions, 478,674 outputs across three policies, 
and 1,436,022 endpoint cells. They add zero independent observations. Capacity counts output IDs; 
actual tokens, tools, and authoring costs are unmeasured.
The fixed both-input annotation-sufficient stratum has 12,067 pairs, 308 tasks, and 203 components. 
It uses complete original inputs and cannot substitute for held-out Q qualification. Aggregation 
reports pair-pooled, equal-task, and equal-component means separately. Full, first-three, and exact 
transitions retain gain, loss, tie, and fallback denominators; false guards remain visible even when 
falling back produces a score tie.
A pre-execution plan, two code files, and six consumed sources are hash-frozen. A separately authored 
implementation reconstructs every output, endpoint cell, and aggregate using scheduled first occur­
rences and bitmasks. It checks 3,046 authored input pairs across 21,322 pair-cap shapes, including 
2,112 explicit rejected-guard counterfamilies, and rejects altered copied inputs and outputs. These 
are same-author checks, not external replication or independent reference families. Supplement S21 
distinguishes this new pass from the preserved earlier failed verifier.

5 Results
Table 1 compares transitions to the same round-robin baseline. In the sufficient-input stratum, 
guarded full-containment losses are zero at every capacity. At cap one, fallback removes 1,409 
promotion losses while sacrificing two of its 565 gains; 563 gains remain. Cap two retains 95 gains 
without its 25 losses. Cap three repairs two promotion losses but ties round-robin throughout: it adds 
no gain over baseline. Fallback counts are 1,502, 29, and two respectively.
The conditional premise matters. Among all pairs, the guard retains 487 cap-one losses despite 2,361 
fallbacks. A minimal authored illustration is A=(0,1), B=(1), with reference {0}. Retaining all of B 
accepts promotion (1), while round-robin (0) succeeds. B is insufficient, so this is outside the theorem. 
The saved bank also contains this failure shape. Calling the policy unconditionally safe would be false.
At caps four through six, full-containment transitions tie, but guarded first-three scoring retains two 
losses. For A=(1,2,0), B=(4,5,0), and alternatives {1,2} or {4,5}, promotion (0,1,4,2) restores full 
containment at four while its first three still fail. Figure 2 preserves the original endpoint separation; 
Supplement S18 visualizes the added guard. Exact scores are separately reported, without inheriting 
the theorem.
Weighting remains consequential. Unprotected promotion at cap one in the fixed sufficient-input 
stratum has pooled contrast -844/12067, approximately -0.06994, but equal-component contrast 
+3363459013/266277211200, approximately +0.01263. A policy cannot choose its preferred weighting 
retrospectively. These exposed finite contrasts estimate neither natural pair prevalence nor behavioral 
composition effects.
The inherited completion-rank identity explains fixed-order full-containment transitions: the first 
successful capacity is the minimum, across alternatives, of their last required ID rank. It does 
not automatically apply to a cap-dependent policy switch. The earlier verifier mistakenly asserted 
guaranteed success for disjoint sufficient inputs; its frozen failure and corrected no-loss check remain 
preserved. This revision changes neither those results nor their correction history.

6 Limitations
The guarantee is about complete-input annotation sufficiency and full containment, not semantic 
truth, actual Q-qualified skills, arbitrary output policies, first-three, or exact matching. Its necessity 
quantifies over all admissible families; actual annotations may permit a rejected promotion. Stable 
class order, equal output count, and the declared union construction matter for the length corollary. 
Compatibility of natural-language requirements needs separate validation.
Independent A/B histories, Q-qualified skill pairs, reserved-S outcomes, native-method runs, and 
measured token costs remain absent; the behavioral effect is null. Future evaluation must freeze source-
disjoint D/Q/S, candidate rights, resets, total cost, and rejection before fresh outcomes. FollowBench 
and ManyIFEval offer constraint controls, not missing skill qualification. SkillsBench supplies native 
task environments, not evidence that this guard improves agents. Supplement S23 states prospective 
gates and falsification tests.
7 Conclusion
A reference-blind structural guard exactly characterizes conditional full-containment safety for this 
binary switch. Its measured benefit is bounded by conservative lost gains, endpoint mismatch, 
and all-pair failures. Transparent accounting preserves those limits while separating a reproducible 
evidence-contract result from the independently qualified behavioral study still required.
