ProbeFlow: Frozen Policies, Changing Preferences
Research Forge · 19 October 2026 · Layout v21 · Research content v21
Development paper v21, 2026-10-19. New exact preference diagnostics on already exposed saved evidence; no new 
independent sample, model call, policy selection, physical experiment or external peer review.
Abstract
Adaptive acquisition must survive a strong fixed-source comparison under an explicit loss and cost 
contract. We analyze frozen policies on one hydraulic rig with 102 policy-fit cycles, 102 develop­
ment cycles, and 421 previously exposed evaluation cycles. An oracle-separated diagnostic locates 
constraints without treating hindsight gains as learnable improvements. New per-cycle checks show 
identical component and complete-diagnosis error counts for the primary TS1 and TS1-plus-PS1 
leaves on every evaluation cycle; the larger mask only adds a source quote. TS1 therefore weakly 
dominates this leaf pair for every convex mixture of the two error rates and every nonnegative 
equal-source cost weight. A secondary frozen gate reduces 63 component errors and 324 quotes but 
adds ten complete-diagnosis failures. Its exact preference boundary separates that genuine tradeoff 
from the primary conditional obstruction. Neither retrospective reweighting, repeated diagnostics 
nor homogeneous policy-fit labels establish independent generalization, physical savings, or native-
method superiority.
1 Introduction
Active feature acquisition chooses which information to buy before predicting. Policy complexity 
is useful only when its apparent gain survives comparison with an affordable, strong fixed source. 
Otherwise a favorable contrast may reflect comparator weakness, predictor changes, or a different 
loss. ProbeFlow asks a narrower diagnostic question: given a frozen predictor and an already fitted 
observation-dependent gate, where does its remaining objective gap originate? The goal is to identify 
what a subsequent experiment must change, rather than introduce another acquisition optimizer.
Our public-data setting uses PS1, FS1, and TS1 records from hydraulic monitoring [1]. A policy first 
acquires one completed-cycle source mean and then stops or acquires another source. We distinguish 
this executable offline observation boundary from physical online sensing. Figure 1 preserves the 
historical support-first rationale. The new question is whether a frozen contrast changes sign when 
the importance of component errors, complete diagnoses and source quotes changes. A conditional 
dominance certificate and a preference boundary answer different parts of that question.

2 Related Work
AFA spans model-based, model-free, and hybrid policies [13]. Joint acquire-or-classify methods [8, 
21] and hierarchical costly features [20] connect classification to information prices. EDDI [12] and 
generative surrogates [9] model missing observations; learned mutual-information selection supplies 
a stronger greedy comparator [10]. Learning-To-Measure [2], BRiG-AFA [3], offline source planning 
[4], and pathwise policy gradients [5] address predictor coupling or future consequences. Our small 
gate is neither a native implementation nor an empirical replacement for these methods.
Offline acquisition validity depends on observation and deployment assumptions [11]; adaptive-
submodularity guarantees require their stated distribution and objective properties [7]. Selection bias 
and structured dependence also constrain interpretation [14–16]. Selective prediction distinguishes 
risk from coverage [6, 17, 18], while loss-dependent decisions explain why component and joint 
errors can disagree [19]. We retain these distinctions without transferring external guarantees or 
benchmark scores. All 21 references and their inherited scopes remain preserved. The v21 resource 
ledger additionally records a selected official AFAPE body review [11] and three candidate dataset 
contracts; none was downloaded or run as a new benchmark.
3 Problem and Method
A mask is a source set: PS1=1, FS1=2, TS1=4. Each acquired source permits its predefined 
terminal features; the policy's branch observes only the purchased root mean. The inspected decision 
interface receives the immutable policy, acquired mask, and disclosed means. Labels, cycle identities, 
unrequested values, and the prediction bank are excluded. A private executor validates affordability 
and charges each source once. After STOP, the terminal mask selects an already cached prediction 
from the same fixed k-neighbor predictor.
Let L_i(m) be four-head component errors divided by 4, C(m) the sum of declared source prices, and 
B a per-cycle hard cap. The feasible set includes every mask satisfying that cap, including the empty 
mask with its frozen fallback. Lambda controls error–quote tradeoffs within feasibility; it cannot 
purchase permission to exceed B. Quote units are declared proxies, not measured latency, energy, 
or money. Every oracle comparison holds the predictor k, feasible masks, prices, B, lambda, and 
cohort fixed.
Write F for the locked fixed-policy mean and G for the actual gate mean. A is the per-cycle all-
feasible-mask oracle mean; P restricts that hindsight choice to the gate's two frozen leaves. Thus A≤F 
and A≤P≤G. Adding and subtracting A and P gives the exact accounting identity below. E includes 
mandatory-root, leaf-set, and price constraints; R uses labels and unobserved predictions. Neither is 
a pure learning error or causal effect. Positive R does not prove an observable policy can attain P.
The distinction between hindsight and executable choice is essential. The all-mask oracle may select 
a source that the gate can never reach, whereas the pair oracle retains the mandatory root and 
its purchase cost. A policy cannot replace either oracle's privileged label access with its current 
confidence. Even a positive difference between G and P combines imperfect branching, restricted 
observations, and unavoidable uncertainty. Establishing a learnable gain would require a separately 
trained observable selector and an evaluation that did not help construct it.
For the new analysis, let alpha in[0,1] mix the component-error rate and the probability of any head 
error. Add lambda times mean source quote divided by3, with equal prices and the same cap2. Alpha 
specifies a decision preference, not a fitted parameter. Predictions, thresholds, leaves and original 
selections remain fixed throughout the sensitivity calculation; k1 and k3 contrasts are never pooled.

4 Experimental Setup
The frozen predictor was trained on historical cycles 1..1540. Policies use 102 fit cycles 1561..1662, 102 
development cycles 1663..1764, and 421 evaluation cycles 1785..2205:625 existing cycles from one rig. 
The evaluation interval was examined in earlier revisions. Recording policy locks before the current 
2

evaluation replay does not restore blindness or create an independent final. Root means summarize 
completed 60-second records; terminal features also include population standard deviation and slope. 
Actual online availability, overlapping-window costs, and sensor failures were not tested.
The inherited grid contains 90 k/price/cap/lambda contexts and 390 fitted gate-context records. 
Fit-only quartile thresholds and distinct feasible leaf pairs produce small gates; development selects 
best-fixed, gate-only, and adaptive-enabled families with recorded deterministic ties. The primary 
context is k1, equal prices, B2, lambda 1/20; its adaptive-enabled selector retains fixed PS1. We 
separately inspect its forced gate g129 and a predetermined k3, lambda 0 selected-gate example. 
The diagnostic reuses all locked records, producing 1170 gate/cohort rows and 4680 support-stratum 
rows. It performs no new fitting or policy selection.
Within a setting, the fixed and gate families use identical cached terminal predictions and development 
loss. Candidate inclusion guarantees only that adaptive-enabled development loss cannot exceed the 
best fixed development loss under the recorded ordering; it supplies no evaluation guarantee. Across 
settings, differing normalization, lambda, or k changes the comparison contract. We therefore report 
primary and secondary examples separately and never average their differently scaled objectives into 
an overall improvement.
The v21 continuation snapshots nine existing input files before execution. It checks two predetermined 
contrasts, 842 policy-cycle rows, 421 leaf certificates and60 dependent preference cells. A failed 
metadata lookup is preserved separately: historical TS1 was not the primary development-selected 
fixed policy. A new frozen execution verifies TS1 directly from its cache and the prior pair-oracle 
objective; it performs no new selection.

Table 1 reports exact objectives and preserves distinct error denominators. For primary g129, 
the root is TS1, threshold 35.44636666666667, with leaves 5 and 4. On421 cycles its actual objec­

R=11/12630, hence G−F=−287/6315. Component errors fall 876→798, but vector errors remain 391 
and total quotes rise 421→443. A favorable PS1 comparison therefore does not isolate an adaptive-
routing benefit.
For primary k1, masks4 and5 have equal component-error counts and equal vector errors on every 
one of421 cycles; mask5 costs one additional quote. Thus TS1 weakly dominates these two frozen 
leaves pointwise for every alpha in[0,1] and lambda>=0. Actual g129 takes the larger mask22 times, 
giving gate-minus-TS1=22lambda/1263, or11/12630 at the original lambda1/20. At lambda0 the 
scalar losses tie. This directly checked boundary is stronger than the original one-loss oracle equality, 
but remains conditional on this predictor, pair, cohort and equal-price cap2 contract.
The predetermined secondary g301 retains k3, equal prices, cap2 and its original lambda0 selection. 
Fixed and gate counts are924/861 component errors,381/391 vector errors and842/518 quotes. 
Reweighting these unchanged outputs yields delta=(-63+103alpha-432lambda)/1684. At zero cost 
weight the gate helps exactly when alpha<63/103; at lambda1/20 the boundary is423/515. For 
lambda>=5/54 its scalar loss is no worse for any alpha, but the ten extra vector failures remain. This 
is post-hoc sensitivity, not a newly optimized winner or three-objective Pareto dominance. Figure2 
preserves the original scale; SupplementS14-S20 derive the new comparisons.
Every policy-fit target has one tuple, seen in only30/421 evaluation cycles. Primary root means are 
outside the fit min/max interval in392/421 cases. These counts flag extrapolation, not density support 
or causality. In the secondary contrast, component errors improve on73 cycles and worsen on10; 
vector errors worsen on10 and improve on none. Eight vector regressions are inside its original root 
range and two outside. Range membership alone cannot explain those failures. Repeated settings and 
overlapping strata remain dependent diagnostics.

6 Limitations
One rig, chronological dependence, prior evaluation exposure, and homogeneous fit labels prevent 
an independent generalization claim. Complete-cycle masking does not reproduce the timing or 
measurement effects of acquiring another physical sensor. Equal caps do not imply equal realized 
quotes, and those quotes omit offline collection, fitting, tuning, and wall-clock expense. The restricted 
gate class, fixed predictor, and two loss definitions limit every boundary. A root-range indicator alone 
cannot identify distributional support or explain individual prediction errors.
Separate arithmetic verification checked the prior1170 rows/4680 strata and, in the new continua­
tion,1684 policy-cycle aggregates,421 leaf certificates and60 preference cells, including hash/metric 
tamper refusals. These are provenance and finite-computation checks, not independent validation. 
No new predictor forward pass, policy fit, selection, physical acquisition, device or native AFA trial 
was executed. Candidate HAR, drift and multisensor protocols in the supplement remain proposals, 
with observation-time, group-split and license limits.
The negative primary boundary is deliberately useful but local: it can stop one unsupported search 
claim without proving that adaptation is generally futile. A successful next study must defeat the 
strong control under its own locked contract, not merely lower error relative to PS1.
7 Conclusion
ProbeFlow separates a weak-comparator gain, an exact local dominance obstruction and a real 
decision tradeoff. The primary frozen pair adds cost without changing errors on any exposed cycle. 
The secondary contrast exchanges fewer component errors and quotes for more complete-diagnosis 
failures, with an exact preference boundary. Future evidence must change or validate candidate, 
predictor, observation and support assumptions on independent groups; reweighting a saved cohort 
does not supply that evidence.
